About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Information Systems at HEC Lausanne, University of Lausanne, currently on a research visit at the Oxford Internet Institute, University of Oxford. My research examines how artificial intelligence changes the way organisations decide, learn and organise work. I combine online and lab experiments, large-scale digital-trace and text data, machine learning, and agent-based simulation.

Trained first as a mechanical engineer (University of Tehran, EPFL), I work at the intersection of computational social science and human–computer interaction. I pair causal identification in large-scale experiments with NLP, network analysis and computational modelling to study how AI shapes what people believe, share and decide, from misinformation and political attitudes to how AI agents and people learn together. I aim to make this work open and reproducible.

Main quest, ready for submission

Interactions with large language models increase support for refugees

Three pre-registered online experiments testing whether brief dialogues with an LLM shift attitudes toward refugees. Empathy-focused dialogue and immersive role-play produced large effects in the UK, replicated in Kenya, with a third study tracing how effects decay over time. Joint work with LMU Munich and HEC Lausanne, prepared for Nature Human Behaviour.

From tools to actors: How agentic AI reshapes organizational learning

An agent-based model extending March’s (1991) mutual-learning framework to treat agentic AI as a new kind of organisational learner. The knowledge–diversity trade-off turns out to depend on environmental turbulence and can even reverse.
